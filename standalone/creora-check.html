Say what your database is supposed to allow.
Find out what it actually does.

Every tool on the market reads your rules and tells you what your rules say. None of them can tell you a rule is wrong. This asks your live database what it actually hands out, writes the answer in plain words, and lets you mark the lines that were never meant to be there.

A wrong rule doesn't throw an error. It just returns the wrong rows — so it ships, keeps working, and nobody finds out until someone who isn't you has the data. This runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere except your own backend.

    What this did not check — every run, on purpose

    • Writing and deleting. This only reads. A table that refuses reads can still accept writes or deletes from a stranger — proving that safely needs a controlled test, not a public tool.
    • One user reading another user's rows. The most common real leak. It needs two real signed-in accounts to prove, which is the audit, not this.
    • What a function actually does. A function being callable is not the same as being unsafe — but each one is a door on the public internet, and this cannot see inside it.
    • Whether an empty result is safe or just empty. From outside, "a policy hid it" and "the table is empty" look identical.
    • Anything about correctness — logic, races, your dependencies, your hosting.

    A finding here is a fact. The absence of one is not. A check that goes quiet when it finds nothing has taught you something false.
