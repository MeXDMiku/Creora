# What every bootstrapped success actually did in year one

**4 Sep 2026. Researched after the plan in `THE_ANSWER.md` was correctly challenged as
"the pre-AI playbook — build in public and hope someone comes."**

**That challenge was right, and the correction is bigger than the plan.**

---

## The eleven

Mailchimp ($12B, $0 VC) · Atlassian (IPO, $10k credit card) · GitHub ($7.5B, $0 for 4.5
years) · Zoho (₹12,000 crore FY25, $0 ever) · Qualtrics ($70M round after **10 bootstrapped
years**) · Shopify · Basecamp · Plenty of Fish ($575M, one person) · Craigslist · Braintree
($800M) · ConvertKit ($13.2M ARR, $0 VC).

## What they did, counted

| | |
|---|---|
| **Charged money within 90 days** | **10 of 11.** Only Craigslist waited — and took nine years to real revenue. Plenty of Fish's first month was **$5.63**. |
| **Funded by a services business, agency, or day job** | **11 of 11.** The only universal fact in the set. |
| **Raised money to go full-time before revenue** | **0 of 11.** |
| **Got their first ten paying customers from a cold audience of strangers** | **0 of 11.** |
| **Had a free tier at the start** | 3 of 11 — and two of those charged a *different party* than the free user. |
| **Hired a salesperson in year one** | **0 of 11.** Atlassian's entire paid marketing budget was **$100/month.** |
| **Built an audience as a deliberate pre-launch tactic** | **0 of 11.** |
| **Median time to $1M revenue** | **~3 years.** To $10M: **~5 years.** |

## The thing they all actually did

Every one of them **built the tool for their own operational pain inside a business they were
already running, and sold it first to people already in the room.**

- **Mailchimp** — a web design agency whose clients kept asking for help sending newsletters.
  Built from recycled code off a failed greeting-card product. **First customers: the agency's
  own client list.** Charged from launch: $50 per 2,500 emails.
- **Atlassian** — ran an outsourced support business at **$360 per resolved issue**, on $10k of
  credit-card debt. JIRA *was the internal tool they built to run that support desk*, released
  at $800 a copy. Farquhar: *"We weren't venture-backed, so we couldn't spend two years
  developing the software. We had to sell something quickly."*
- **Basecamp** — built to run their own consultancy; first customers were their own consulting
  clients asking *"can we use it too?"* Target was $5,000/month; **hit in six weeks.**
- **Shopify** — built to run their own snowboard shop.
- **Braintree** — Bryan Johnson was a **door-to-door credit-card salesman**. When his
  non-compete expired he **flew to Utah and personally visited his former customers**;
  enough switched to make **$6,200/month**. Priced at $200/month minimum *deliberately, as a
  filter*.
- **Qualtrics** — **cold-called academics** for ten years at ~$40,000/year, no free tier.
  Year one: **20 customers.** Ryan Smith would transfer callers to "research" — his father's
  office down the hall — to sound like a bigger company.
- **Zoho** — contract work for network-equipment vendors funded it. Gave away a free Java SNMP
  API so engineers would download it, then sold **OEM licences to their employers**. 1997
  revenue ~$350,000; ~$10M by 2000.

## And the one that built in public nearly died doing it

**ConvertKit** launched as a public "Web-App Challenge" in January 2013 — the purest
build-in-public story on the list. It stalled at ~$2,000 MRR and then went **backwards, to
$1,330 MRR by October 2014.** Of ten high-profile people Nathan Barry asked to buy, **one did.**

What rescued it: quitting the public narrative, putting in **$50,000 of his own money**,
narrowing to one niche (professional bloggers), and **cold-emailing them one at a time** with
free concierge migration. $5,020 MRR March 2015 → **$97,000 MRR by December 2015.**

## The three modern facts that break the plan in `THE_ANSWER.md`

**1. "Free library first, monetise later" is the documented way to fail.**
**Kite** — an AI coding assistant with **500,000+ monthly active developers** and real VC —
shut down in November 2022 with the diagnosis in its own words: *"our 500k developers would
not pay to use it."* **Beekeeper Studio** posted "10x year-over-year growth" at **16 months
with revenue of exactly zero.** Rob Walling's Bidsketch data: removing the free plan produced
an **8x increase in paid conversions within weeks.**

Every strong 2018+ bootstrapped case — **Plausible, Fathom, Bannerbear, Simple Analytics** —
runs a **trial, not a free tier**, and reached seven figures with one to four people.

Mailchimp, the most famous freemium story in software, **charged for eight years first** and
added the free tier in 2009 *from profitability*. Users went 85,000 → 450,000 in twelve
months and profit rose 650%. Free worked **after** product-market fit, not instead of it.

**2. Individual developers are the wrong buyer.** That is what killed Kite at half a million
users. Every bootstrapped winner sells to a company or a business owner. Conversion for
developer-focused tools is a **median 5% — explicitly half that of tools not sold to
developers.** Sidekiq: 1,850 paying customers against **115 million downloads.**

**3. Open source is distribution, not a business — and only with a real paid product.**
Sidekiq is the best case that exists: **$13.5M lifetime, 1,850 customers, one employee.** But
Perham blogged from **2007**, five years before the product, and his own stated biggest
mistake was *"not selling as a subscription from day one."* Donations are worse than nothing:
one maintainer documented **25,000 GitHub stars producing 3 sponsors**, and among the top
1,000 GitHub maintainers **the median sponsor count is zero.**

## What this means here, concretely

**The correction is not a better free channel. There isn't one.** It is that every founder who
started with nothing had **income from adjacent work**, and that income was both the funding
*and* the customer list.

So the shape is:

1. **Get paid to do the work next to the problem** — contract or freelance work on Supabase
   and AI-built apps. That is the day job that funds it, and it is where the first ten paying
   customers come from. This is what 11 of 11 did.
2. **Build the tool out of that pain.** Already true — the checks in this repo exist because
   this project's own backend needed them, and they found real things.
3. **Charge inside 90 days. Trial, not free tier. Price high enough to filter** — Braintree's
   $200/month minimum was a screen, not an accident.
4. **Sell to the company, never to the individual developer.** Kite is the tombstone.

## Two numbers to hold

**The one that should worry you:** roughly **68% of indie software businesses earn under
$1,000 in their entire lifetime**, and **0.9% ever cross $1M.** Median time to $1M among the
eleven above was **three years**; to $10M, five.

**The one that shouldn't:** **Wingify/VWO** — Delhi, founded 2010, **never raised a rupee** —
reached ~$50M annualised revenue across 6,000 clients in 90 countries and sold a majority
stake for **$200M in January 2025**, founder still holding 71%. **Zoho** crossed **₹12,000
crore** in FY25, bootstrapped from Chennai. **Postman** was built in Bangalore. A low cost
base is a decisive runway advantage, and payment and incorporation friction is solved —
Stripe Atlas now operates from 169 countries.

**Location is not the constraint. Buyer access is — and that is the same constraint a
founder with no audience has in San Francisco.**
